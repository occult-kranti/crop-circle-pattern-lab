Reverse cymatic decoding lab

Cymatics renders vibration modes as visible nodal-line patterns (Chladni figures). This lab runs the chain backwards: each major formation's geometry → candidate vibration modes (n = azimuthal order, m = radial order) → frequency ratios → absolute Hz on assumed reference resonators → nearest musical notes and pairwise "difference vibrations" (beats / combination tones).

Honesty framing. This is a modeling exercise, not evidence that crop circles encode sound. The geometry→mode inversion is fundamentally ambiguous (many mode sets share a symmetry), and absolute Hz depends entirely on the assumed resonator. What is resonator-independent and reported as primary: the dimensionless ratio f/f₀₁. Built with a verified open-source simulator (numpy/scipy, 31/31 tests passing); planned with physics, mathematics and sound-engineering consultants.

Key findings: (1) the early Hampshire glyphs decode to low modes — the dumbbell (1990) sits at G♯4 and the tetrahedron (1991) at E5, an exact-just major 6th apart (−1.6¢); (2) the 2008 pi wheel vs the 2012 3-D cubes land on a tritone within −3¢; (3) spiral "construction counts" (149 circles, 409 circles) are not physical modes and are flagged LOW confidence; (4) the 23×73 Chilbolton grid is the only HIGH-confidence decode — because its mode numbers are printed in the design itself.
